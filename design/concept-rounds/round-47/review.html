integrity-alarm
Integrity alarm (Levels 13 and 17). L.4

dome-hit

Dome hit
dome-hit
Dome hit (Level 17). It plays when an enemy shot hits a Level 17 colony dome, a greenhouse of thick glass. It should sound like glass being hit: sharp, a hard knock with a bright crack, maybe a faint stress creak; not a thud. a: a hard glass impact (a metal object on glass, with cracks forming), the ring of a window pane under it and a sharp glass crack 0.1 s later: bright and hard, 0.9 s (CC0). Note: the main layer's recording is described as made from a plate, foil and ice, not a real pane, so it may sound synthetic. b: a window struck hard: the knock and the pane ringing, a second hard tap's ring on it, and a stress crack in the glass right after (a cooling glass cracking): a glassy 'tock' with a ring, 1 s (CC0, CC-BY 4.0). Your comment that caused the redo: round 46 (K.11): "Neither sound like glass being hit. Glass being hit is a sharp sound, not a thud.". No recommendation (I cannot listen).

Weak spots (Claude) a's main source is made from plate, foil and ice rather than a real pane

Answer redo (the user: "These are too small. a is a bottle cracking, b is nothing like glass breaking. A dome is a very large structure.")
Outcome closed: both rejected; a new redo follows in round 49

launch-rail

Launch rail at every level start.
launch-rail
Launch rail at every level start. It plays for 4.9 s at every level start: the ship is catapulted off the carrier and the shuttle stops at 3.6 s. It should sound like an aircraft-carrier catapult launch: the jet at full power, the run, the hard stop, steam. a: a real carrier launch (USS George H. W. Bush) with a real F-4's afterburner at full power under its start, then the catapult fires at 1.2 s and the jet's roar tears past; at 3.55 s a rail car's hard steel slam (the shuttle hitting its stop) and a steam release hissing as the roar fades (CC0, CC-BY 4.0). b: another real launch from the same carrier: an afterburner at full power, the catapult firing at 1.2 s and the jet's roar rising more slowly; at 3.55 s a train coupler's sharp steel clank over a rail car slam's low body (a brighter, more metallic stop than a), then a close steam release (CC0, CC-BY 3.0, CC-BY 4.0). Your comment that caused the redo: round 46 (K.20): "Neither sounds like a launch. Maybe look for aircraft carrier launch sounds.". No recommendation (I cannot listen).
